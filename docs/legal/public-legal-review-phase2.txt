C. YOUR FOUR OPEN QUESTIONS, ANSWERED FROM THE CODE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

C1. THE LEADERBOARD — and this is the most important item in this review.

    ANSWER: There is NO leaderboard in the public terminal. Every deck reports
    hasLeaderboard:false, there is no board route, and the client hides the tab
    rather than opening an empty one. Nothing about one user is visible to any
    other user anywhere in the public build. You do NOT need an "information
    visible to other users" section.

    BUT THE APPLICATION CURRENTLY SAYS OTHERWISE, AND THAT IS A BLOCKER.
    The public Preferences screen (reachable at my_prefs, in the public nav)
    draws a "Public leaderboards" card that tells the visitor, verbatim:

        "The terminal's built-in study games rank every player on one board.
         Your display name and score are visible to anyone playing the same
         game."

    That is false in the public terminal. The screen is shared with the private
    portal, where it is true, and it was never filtered out of the public build.

    So the application is making an untrue statement about who can see a user's
    data — the same class of problem as an aspirational privacy policy, pointed
    the other way. Publishing a policy that correctly says nothing is shared,
    while a settings screen says a stranger can see your name and score, is
    worse than either alone.

    I am removing that card from the public build. Treat this as a NOT YET TRUE
    item in reverse: the policy's silence on user-to-user visibility is correct
    and must not change, and the app has to stop contradicting it.

C2. CAN A URL HANDED TO TRADINGVIEW CARRY AN ACCOUNT IDENTIFIER?

    ANSWER: No. TvWidget appends a script whose src is a fixed TradingView URL
    and whose body is a JSON config of symbols and display options. No account
    id, no email, no user content is in it. The public terminal routes by URL
    hash and its routes carry no identifiers, so the Referer TradingView sees is
    a bare page URL with a screen name in the fragment.

    Your Section 5.3 list is accurate as written and does not need widening.

C3. DOES TRADINGVIEW DISPLAY REAL MARKET INFORMATION?

    ANSWER: Yes, and your catch is correct. The chart and watchlist tiles are
    live TradingView components showing TradingView's own market data, including
    in the demo. "Every figure in the demo is fabricated" is true of VTS's own
    account, trade, portfolio and performance figures and is NOT true of what
    those two tiles render.

    Your drafts already draw this distinction and I would keep the wording you
    used. My fact sheet should have caught it.

C4. WHAT ACCOUNT DELETION HAS TO REACH.

    Better news than you assumed. Of the account-linked tables, 26 carry
    "references public_accounts(id) on delete cascade" and 2 carry
    "on delete set null". So deleting the account row removes the user's data
    from 26 tables by database action rather than by application code
    remembering to.

    Two things are NOT covered and must be handled explicitly:
      - public_early_access_requests has no foreign key to the account. This is
        the row we agreed to delete by matching email address.
      - public_rate_limits has no account column at all — it is keyed by bucket
        and hash and holds nothing identifying now that the IP is hashed.

    So the deletion route is: delete the early-access row by email, delete the
    account row, let the cascade do the rest, all in one transaction. I will
    verify it against the real database and report the row counts rather than
    asserting it works.

D. WHERE I DISAGREE WITH THE DRAFTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D1. THE 180-DAY RETENTION PROMISE IS THE MOST LIKELY SENTENCE IN EITHER DOCUMENT
    TO BECOME FALSE.

    You flagged this yourself and then wrote it into the policy as a positive
    statement anyway. Nothing in the codebase deletes anything on a schedule
    today — there is no cron, no sweep, no job runner in the public service at
    all. A promise to delete automatically is not a wording choice, it is a
    feature.

    I am not arguing against the period. I am arguing that the sentence must not
    exist until the job does. I will build the sweep in the same change as
    deletion, and then the sentence is true and can stay exactly as you wrote it.

D2. SECTION 8.3 IS HONEST AND I WOULD STILL RATHER DELETE IT.

    "Expired authentication rows are not currently removed through an automatic
    scheduled sweep" is accurate and I respect writing it down. But it publishes
    a weakness to solve a problem that a small amount of code solves better.
    Once the sweep in D1 exists, expiring authentication rows can ride the same
    job and that sentence goes away.

    If you disagree — if there is a reason to prefer disclosing it over fixing
    it — say so and I will leave it.

D3. THE CLICKWRAP ACCEPTANCE RECORD IS RIGHT AND I WOULD ADD NOTHING.

    Storing a SHA-256 of the exact accepted text, append-only, versioned, with
    no raw IP, is a better design than I would have specified. I will build it
    as written.

D4. AGE 18 AND UNITED-STATES-ONLY ARE NOT ENFORCED BY ANY CODE, AND SHOULD NOT
    BE.

    Nothing in the codebase asks for or checks a date of birth or a country, and
    I would not add either — collecting a birthdate to enforce an age rule means
    collecting a birthdate, which is more personal data than the rule saves.

    Admission is entirely manual through the `admit` command, so Andrew IS the
    enforcement mechanism. That is a real control, not an absent one, but it is
    a control he has to actually apply. Your placement of these under NOT YET
    TRUE is right for that reason, and the fix is a decision rather than a build.
